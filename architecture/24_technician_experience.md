# Technician Experience

Version: 2.0  
Status: Architecture Baseline

## Purpose

The technician experience supports safe field judgment, clear commitments, reliable evidence, and low-friction reconstruction under real conditions.

## Work view

Technicians see accepted commitments, organizational priority, due boundaries, hazards, access constraints, relevant asset/history, current hypotheses, required capability, and evidence/verification criteria. Organizational Priority and deadline remain visible while the technician controls practical Execution Sequence within constraints.

## Field workflow

The interface supports accept/decline with reason, travel/access, inspect, record observations, propose hypotheses, stabilize, perform work, pause/handover, capture materials and evidence, and request verification. It separates observed facts from diagnosis and from actions taken.

## Emergency and offline operation

An emergency action starts with a minimal safety record or none when even that is unsafe. Offline capture keeps local event/action times and durable identifiers, then synchronizes without duplicating work. Post-stabilization reconstruction asks only knowable details and permits uncertainty, danger, device failure, and witness accounts as explicit context.

## Interruptions

When critical work interrupts planned work, the technician records safe stopping state and affected commitment. The manager or standing emergency policy supplies authority for reprioritization. The previous task remains visible with a recovery plan.

## Verification and learning

Technicians can recommend completion but cannot solely verify their own high-risk work. Failed verification is actionable feedback, not erased history. Context-bound lessons are suggested with asset and environment mismatches exposed.

## Human factors

Controls are glove-friendly, accessible, low bandwidth, and concise. Location tracking is purpose-limited and transparent. Metrics do not covertly rank personnel without governance and review.
