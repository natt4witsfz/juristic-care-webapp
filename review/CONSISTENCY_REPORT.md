## Concept separation check

Responsibility is represented by effective-dated ledger entries. Authority is represented by mandates and policy sources. Commitment records accepted work. Capability records competence. Availability records ability to act during a period. Organizational Priority is an authorized management decision. Execution Sequence is a technician's practical ordering. SLA is a versioned organizational service expectation. None implies another, and role permissions do not manufacture business authority.

## Scenario checks

1. Five similar Reports create five Cases; later human investigation may associate all five with one Incident.
2. Recurrence after two days or six months triggers contextual investigation, not automatic reopen or automatic new Incident.
3. Emergency work outside office hours proceeds within capability and necessity, then records actual event/action/record/upload chronology.
4. Critical work may interrupt planned work while preserving the paused commitment, authority/rationale, consequences, and recovery plan.
5. Danger or device failure may delay evidence without blocking stabilization; the limitation is recorded later.
6. Remote verification is allowed only under risk policy, qualification, authority, sufficient evidence, and stated limitations.
7. Work discovered outside the system is reconstructed with provenance and is never backdated as prior approval.
8. New evidence creates revised understanding and a superseding decision without changing earlier records.
9. Successors receive context and future stewardship without inheriting predecessors' moral responsibility.
10. AI suppresses confidence when historical asset or environmental context no longer matches.
11. Embarrassing history cannot be removed merely by committee request; lawful restriction or disposal remains independently authorized and auditable.
12. Export, schema documentation, ADRs, restore tests, and ownership rules preserve continuity after all original people and suppliers leave.

All scenarios pass at the architecture level.

## Terminology and lifecycle checks

The glossary is canonical. Case, Incident, and Operation are not used interchangeably. Event names describe accepted past facts. Lifecycle transitions preserve prior close and decision states. Evidence is assessed for relevance and reliability but never treated as an automatic truth score.

## Security and privacy consistency

Least privilege, purpose limitation, tenant isolation, redacted views, immutable authoritative records, audited disclosure, lawful disposition, emergency access review, and supplier exit requirements align across security, evidence, permissions, data, AI, and the reference architecture.
