# Resident Experience

Version: 2.0  
Status: Architecture Baseline

## Purpose

Residents need a safe, understandable way to report concerns and follow outcomes without being exposed to internal or private operational details.

## Experience flow

Residents describe what they observed, impact, approximate time/location, safety concern, contact preference, and optional media. Submission immediately creates a distinct Case and acknowledgement. Similar reports may be acknowledged as possibly related only after authorized determination; the resident's Case remains independently traceable.

The Case view shows received time, current service stage, requests for information, appropriate appointments, public-safe progress, outcome, and how to challenge or reopen. It distinguishes the resident's urgency opinion from Organizational Priority.

## Safety

Immediate-danger guidance directs people to emergency services or safe action before form completion. Media is optional when capture could increase risk. Sensitive location, identity, household, or medical details are minimized and purpose-protected.

## Communication rules

Plain-language updates explain what changed, not merely a status label. Closure communicates what was found, what was done, remaining limitations, and how recurrence will be assessed. A resident can add new evidence after closure; the system preserves it and routes human review rather than silently reopening or dismissing it.

## Edge cases

Anonymous or proxy reports follow policy while preserving source limits. Multiple residents using one device still create separate Cases. Accidental duplicate submission may be marked as such by a human but is not deleted. Accessibility, language, low bandwidth, and non-digital assisted intake are supported.

## Future evolution

New channels must preserve the same Case identity, consent, chronology, and acknowledgement guarantees. Resident satisfaction is interpreted alongside access and outcome measures, never as the sole truth of performance.
